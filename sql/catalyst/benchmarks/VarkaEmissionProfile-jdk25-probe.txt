The emission profile (VARKA-191.md 2): VarkaEmissionBenchmark's wide four-op shape -
greatest(greatest(add_months(d, k), date_add(d, k)), last_day(d)) for k = 1..n over one date
column - compiled and emitted repeatedly by VarkaEmitDump --repeat under a JFR recording
(settings=profile), and each execution sample attributed by dev/varka_jfr_frames.py to the
first emitter phase whose frame the stack holds. The byte budget is set out of reach at every
width, as the benchmark sets it at 200 and 400, so every rung is one build with no regroup. The
time per emission is the probe's reading on a laptop at a load of three to five, one fork, and
not the benchmark's number; the shares and their growth are what this file is for.

Reproduce (each width):
  java -XX:StartFlightRecording=filename=emit.jfr,settings=profile ... VarkaEmitDump <n exprs> \
    --columns d:date --options methodByteBudget=1048576 --repeat <20000 / n>
  jfr print --events jdk.ExecutionSample --stack-depth 64 emit.jfr | dev/varka_jfr_frames.py

## 4. The profile again, after the fix, with the probe corrected (VARKA-191.md 9)

Sections 1 to 3 above were read through VarkaEmitDump, which handed the emitter its outputs as a
Scala List seen through asJava - a list whose get(i) walks from the head. The planner indexes the
outputs per method, so every emission above also paid a walk per lookup, which inflated the times
(12.4, 43.2 and 205.8 ms) and planning's share (35.1%, 56.5%, 71.8%). Production was never
affected: the shape cache copies the outputs into a random-access list. VarkaLoopEmitter.emit now
copies its outputs on entry, so every caller gets the same, and the probe below is rerun with that
fix, in both forms. Its times now agree with VarkaEmissionBenchmark's to within the probe's JFR
overhead; the benchmark's files are the numbers.
