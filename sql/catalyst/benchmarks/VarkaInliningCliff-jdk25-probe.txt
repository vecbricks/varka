Task 209's admission check (PLAN_TASK_209.md 2 and 9; the budget's own forks in section 5, 11 and
12 there): does the loop method of a wide kernel keep
its vector intrinsics, and does the same class land on either side from one JVM to the next?

Taken 27 September 2026 on the laptop (AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX PRO 370, OpenJDK 25.0.4.1), unpinned,
with dev/varka_inlining_cliff.sh: fresh JVMs of VarkaInliningCliffProbe, task 198's cheap-tail
shape `year(d) + k` in one loop method (fused ceiling 400) or split into groups (100, 50), each
fork run for six seconds over 1024-row batches, under -XX:+LogCompilation and PrintInlining and
PrintIntrinsics scoped to the emitted class; six seconds a fork in the first census, and
twenty-four in the second run of the boundary cases, whose reader also says the first second
from which a fork stayed fast. A fork's verdict is the median rate of its last three seconds,
slow above 40 nanoseconds a row; the two sides are about 1 to 7 and 115 to 290.
The compile logs were read with dev/varka_inlining_cliff.py and dev/varka_c2_report.py. Every
arm's directory under target/varka-inlining-cliff/ holds its session log, the per-fork compile
logs and its summary; this file is the summaries.
