================================================================================================
Milestone 4 pre-registration measurements (JMH)
================================================================================================

Five candidate-lowering comparisons for VARKA-27, VARKA-28 and VARKA-30, each over one N=4096 row buffer
(one Spark COLUMN_BATCH_SIZE, L1/L2-resident). See VarkaMilestone4MeasurementsBenchmark.java for
what each pair measures and why.

This file is the forked-JVM, species-isolated measurement that replaced four in-process
(forks=0) runs, which are in git history at a092f66d096 with their own reading. Two things were
wrong with that harness (m4/PLAN.md, debt register): every benchmark ran on a JVM the
JUnit suites and the earlier benchmarks had already shaped, and this class's single @Setup ran
the lane-width pair - the one measurement that uses a second int species, ISPEC_HALF - before
every benchmark, which turns the shared IntVector templates bimorphic and can leave a heap box
per loop iteration (SKILLS.md, "Every operator the plans rely on"). Each benchmark below ran in
its own JVM, and only laneWidthNarrowestDrive's JVM ever saw the second species. What that
changed is listed at the end.

OpenJDK 64-Bit Server VM 25.0.4+7-1-26.04-Ubuntu on Linux 7.0.0-30-generic
AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX PRO 370 w/ Radeon 890M (AVX-512 on a 256-bit datapath), machine otherwise idle
JMH 1.37, throughput mode, forks=1 (one forked JVM per benchmark, launched from the surefire
JVM with its argLine; the narrow run's fork inherits -XX:MaxVectorSize=16), 1 thread,
3 x 2 s warmup, 5 x 2 s measurement. Higher is better.
Run: ./build/mvn -f sql/varka/engine/pom.xml test -Dvarka.jmh=true

--- Reading these numbers (ratios by minimum-of-5) ---

* Alignment: aligned beats misaligned at both widths - 1.54x at AVX-512 (8351 against 5430),
  1.24x at 128-bit (4403 against 3552). Real, and it scales with width: bigger where a load
  spans more of a cache line.
* Boolean materialization (a single comparison): the two materializing forms are no longer
  tied. toVector+and beats blend by 1.12x at AVX-512 (7486 against 6692); at 128-bit blend is
  ahead by 1.04x (3807 against 3662), inside noise. The width-dependent finding stands exactly
  as before: not materializing at all (bits-only, mask.toLong() straight into the packed bitmap)
  wins by 1.18x at AVX-512 (8868 against toVector's 7486) and *loses* at 128-bit, 1.34x to
  toVector and 1.39x to blend (2740 against 3662 and 3807). The strategy must not be picked once
  and assumed to generalize across widths.
* Lane-width conversion: narrowest-drive and part-loop stay tied - narrowest-drive 1.02x ahead
  at AVX-512 (2831 against 2767), part-loop 1.02x ahead at 128-bit (2050 against 2014). This
  pair is the only one measured with two int species in its JVM, and only in
  narrowest-drive's fork; the design decision (VARKA-39) is the two-part convertShape from the
  preferred species regardless, because a second species poisons every other IntVector loop in
  the same JVM.
* Boolean trees ((a > b) AND (c < d)): mask-space is ahead at both widths but by little - 1.02x
  at AVX-512 (3590 against 3504) and 1.07x at 128-bit (1305 against 1215). Never worse, so
  the rule (walk boolean sub-expressions in mask space, materialize once at the output boundary)
  keeps its justification; the 1.24x-1.37x margin the in-process runs reported at 128-bit was
  the harness, not the kernels.
* Division safety: blend-then-div beats masked-div at both widths, and by more than the
  in-process runs showed - 1.23x at AVX-512 (157.4 against 128.2), 1.31x at 128-bit (84.1
  against 64.3). Consistent in direction with the earlier runs and no longer under the 1.3x
  line at the narrow width.

--- What moved against the in-process runs (runs 3-4 at a092f66d096, mean-of-5) ---

* Same conclusion, similar numbers: alignment (1.56-1.79x then, 1.54x now at AVX-512),
  bits-only versus materializing at both widths, lane-width tie, unroll (other file).
* Changed: toVector+and against blend, "statistically tied" then, 1.12x apart at AVX-512 now;
  boolTree mask-space at 128-bit, 1.24x-1.37x then, 1.07x now; div blend-then-div, 1.08x-1.10x
  at AVX-512 then, 1.23x now. All three are shapes with blends or masked ops, which is where
  a bimorphic template costs a box; the plain add and the mask-to-long shapes did not move.
* boolTreeMaterializedInt at 128-bit rose from 1050 to 1215 ops/ms; addMisaligned at AVX-512
  from 4822 to 5430; divMaskedDiv at AVX-512 fell from 141 to 128. Where a number fell, the
  in-process run had been measuring a warmer profile than a fresh JVM gets, not a faster kernel.
