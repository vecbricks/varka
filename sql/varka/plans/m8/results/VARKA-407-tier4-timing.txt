VARKA-407: timing the 64-tail ladder's arms in compiled code, and what a real session's first queries run.
Run 20 October 2026 on the laptop (AMD Zen 5, JDK 25.0.4.1), JVM pinned `taskset -c 0,12,13,14,15,1,2,3`,
both agent locks held, java launched directly with the Test javaOptions of `show catalyst/Test/javaOptions`.
Cells are Per Row (ns) of VarkaSharedPrefixBenchmark's first shape, the four "prefix computed once" ceilings
(400/200/100/50) and "every group split"; "call-site budget off" (always 240 to 490; 238.8 once) is left out. A cell over
20 ns/row is slow. Fast mode 2.6 at 512 bits, 4.0 at 256 bits (-XX:MaxVectorSize=32).
A "run" is one whole benchmark JVM; alternated means a 512-bit run and a 256-bit run in turn.

== phase 1: the benchmark change alone, and the warm-up change alone ==
Variants (runs with a slow cell / runs):
  master c6193e3c39b, no change             512 bit  2 / 5 (one unalternated)   256 bit  3 / 4
  harness: every arm driven until its drive time is steady with C2 idle, all arms before the
  first is timed (an earlier version of the change)       512 bit  1 / 5 (+1 of 6 with a JIT log)   256 bit  1 / 5
  warm-up change alone (a wait at the warm-up's end, up to 1 s, until C2 has nothing queued and
  nothing compiling; the benchmark unchanged)             512 bit  4 / 5      256 bit  3 / 5
  harness: each arm driven that way at its own turn, just before it is timed (the committed change)
                                                          512 bit  0 / 5      256 bit  0 / 5
The warm-up change does not remove the slow cells (7 of 10 runs slow, against 5 of 9 without it), and it adds
up to a second to a session's start, so it is not part of this change. The waiting for C2 at the warm-up's end
cannot reach the slow window either: the warm-up runs once, seconds into the JVM, and the one logged slow arm's request
came at 54 s, 17 to 54 s into the JVM (VARKA-406-slow-mode.txt). The first version of the harness change left slow cells
because an arm warmed minutes before its turn was found slow again: in the one JIT log of such a run (a
LogCompilation log, cells "ceiling 200" 12.4 and "ceiling 100" 69.5), the arm "ceiling 100" had C2 code at 27
to 29 s and was compiled again from tier 2 at 65 s, in its timed window, with no deoptimisation or make-not-entrant
record for the C2 code in the log; why the C2 code went is not established. Driving each arm at its own turn
is a design choice that leaves no idle gap between the drives and the timing; the data does not separate it from
the earlier version (3 of 16 runs slow against 0 of 10), only both from master (5 of 9 against 0 of 10).

== phase 3: do a real session's first queries run in tier 3 after the warm-up? ==
Method: a local[2] session (VarkaArrowSessions.createSession, Varka on, the kernel warm-up off), 100,000 cached
dates with one null in 31 (so the kernels run their masked loops), the same query every time over 1, 4 and 16
entries of the size ladder, 60 to 100 queries, -XX:+PrintCompilation, master's catalyst jar and the jar with the
warm-up wait of phase 1 (16 and 4 entries on both jars, which agree; 1 entry on the change jar only). The
template warm-up logged "took 232 to 366 ms ... C2's queue was 0 before the rounds and at most 6 to 10 while
paced". Times are JVM uptime in ms.
  1 entry:   query 0 at 14.4 s; loopMasked0 tier 3 (OSR at 15,029, standard at 15,064); tier 4 OSR at 15,434
             (0.4 s later); tier 4 standard at 15,568. Tier-3 code for about 0.4 s, then C2.
  4 entries: loopMasked0 (3,693 bytes) tier-3 OSR at 14,734 is refused by C1 ("out of virtual registers in LIR
             generator"); tier 4 OSR at 15,163 (0.43 s later). Interpreted for 0.4 s, then C2.
  16 entries: the four loopMasked methods (3.5 to 3.8 KB) are refused by C1 the same way at 15,602..15,763;
             tier 4 OSR at 16,675..16,853 for three of them (1.0 s later) and for the fourth at 18,505 or
             19,332 (2.7 to 3.6 s later). Interpreted for 1.0 to 3.6 s, then C2. (The kernel's first
             queries before that run the row path while the kernel is built; query 0 is at 14.1 s.)
  At 4 and 16 entries the OSR code is later made not entrant by an uncommon trap (16.7 s; 18.6 to 20.3 s) and
  recompiled at tier 4; at 1 entry the tier-3 code is made not entrant by the tier-4 install.
Answer: no late tier-4 request. A session's first queries run a kernel loop in C1 code (1 entry) for about
0.4 s, or in the interpreter where C1 refuses the loop (4 and 16 entries) for 0.4 to 1.0 s, and then in C2
code, with and without the wait at the warm-up's end. The 17 to 54 s tier-3 window is a property of the
benchmark, which drives an arm for 977 calls and leaves it idle for minutes; it is not a production cost.
One thing the logs show that is not this row's: C1 refused every masked loop of 3.5 KB and more that was seen (five
methods), so those loops run interpreted until C2's OSR compile lands, 0.4 to 3.6 s after they get hot (proposed as a row).
