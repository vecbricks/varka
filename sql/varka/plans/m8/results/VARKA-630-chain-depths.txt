VARKA-630 part 4: how deep a nested chain fuses through the SQL compile path with the chain-depth
cap raised to 64, and what stops it.

Date: 21 November 2026, one laptop, one JVM, a scratch test run once per cell (single readings,
not a benchmark): `VarkaExpressionCompiler.explainPredicate` over `(CHAIN) > c` on the master of
d49480da0fe, options `VarkaMatrixRuns.BASE.withMaxChainDepth(64)`, a 30 s bound that no cell
approached. The scratch test is not kept; it is the loop the two new tests in
VarkaVectorIRSharingSuite run, over the depths listed.

Each row is a chain over a column a, nested by substituting the previous level for X:

  chain            paths per level   fuses at depth   first depth that declines, and why
  signum(X)        4                 12               16: "exceeds the emitter's fused budget"
  abs(X) (ym)      3                 28               32: "exceeds the emitter's fused budget"
  (X) / 3 (ym)     7                 4                8:  "exceeds the emitter's fused budget"
  (X + 1), INT     1                 63               64: "exceeds the emitter's fused budget"
  (X + 1), BIGINT  1                 62               63: "exceeds the emitter's fused budget"

Every explanation came back in under 800 ms (the first, 721 ms, is class loading), the rest in
under 200 ms, and a declined one in under 40 ms, whatever the depth, up to 64 levels, which is
2^64 paths for a chain of fan-out two.

The decline is the node budget, `VarkaEmitBudget.MAX_FUSED_NODES` = 64 distinct operation
nodes, not the chain-depth cap: the cap was 64 here. The budget stops every chain above before 64 levels, and the INT add chain, the cheapest, one
level short of 64; the BIGINT add chain stops a level earlier, and the reason text shows its
literal is cast, which would be one more node a level. The nodes per level of the other chains
were not counted.

What this means for the chain-depth cap (row 629): raising the INT lane's cap to 64 cannot let a
query fuse a 64-deep chain through the compiler, since the node budget stops it at 63 for the
cheapest chain there is. The depth benchmark (`VarkaChainDepthBenchmark`) reached 64 because it
builds the IR directly and does not pass the compiler's budget. A row that needs a chain of 26 to
60 levels of decimal operations, each several nodes, is stopped by the node budget at a depth
the cap would allow.

Not measured: the emitter's own class size and the cache hit of a chain that fuses at these depths
(a chain at the budget's edge was not emitted here), and the BIGINT and DOUBLE chains of the
plan's decimal rows.
