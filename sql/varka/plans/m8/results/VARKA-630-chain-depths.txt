VARKA-630 part 4: how deep a nested chain fuses through the SQL compile path, with the chain-depth
cap at 64 and at 128, and what stops it.

Date: 21 November 2026, one laptop, one JVM, a scratch test run once per cell (single readings,
not a benchmark; the loops are in VARKA-630-chain-depths.scratch.txt, rewritten from the
scratch tests that were run and not run again, and the two new tests in VarkaVectorIRSharingSuite pin
some of the cells): `VarkaExpressionCompiler.explainPredicate` over
`(CHAIN) > c` on master d49480da0fe, options `VarkaMatrixRuns.BASE.withMaxChainDepth(cap)`.

Each chain nests by substituting the previous level for X. "fuses" is the deepest depth read
that fuses, "declines" the first read that does not (the depths read are listed, not every one).

  chain             paths  cap 64: fuses / declines    cap 128: fuses / declines
  signum(X) double  4      12 / 16                     16 / 24
  abs(X) ym         3      28 / 32                     40 / 64
  (X + 1) INT       1      63 / 64                     64 / 100
  (X + 1) BIGINT    1      62 / 63                     63 / 100
  (X) / 3 ym        7      4 / 8                       not read at cap 128

Depths read at cap 64: signum 4 to 64, abs 4 to 64, / 3 4 to 64, the adds 16 to 72 (INT and
BIGINT). At cap 128: signum 12, 16, 24, 40; abs 28, 32, 40, 64; the adds 63, 64, 100, 126, 127
and 130 (INT) and 62, 63, 100, 126 and 127 (BIGINT).

What stops a chain at cap 64 is the chain-depth cap: at cap 128 every chain read stops somewhere
else, further on, and the add chains reach the cap's edge at 64 (one level short of it for the INT
chain, which fits the predicate's compare being a level of the chain; not checked). At cap 128
the stops are not all the cap's: the INT add chain stops at 89 by the method byte budget (below),
and the signum and abs stops at cap 128 (16 / 24 and 40 / 64) were not attributed to either limit,
nor was the `/ 3` chain read there. In the table, "declines" is the first depth read that did not
fuse, not the stop itself. The decline's text, "exceeds the emitter's fused budget", is the one
note the compiler gives whatever budget failed, depth included, so it does not say which.
Neither is `VarkaEmitBudget.MAX_FUSED_NODES` the limit: it is checked only where
`methodByteBudget` is 0 (`Analysis` line 1953) and the default is 8000.

A second limit appears past the cap, and it is the method byte budget. At cap 128 the INT add
chain declined at 100 levels, well under the cap; a bisection (cap 256, the same predicate) puts
the stop at 89 levels fused and 90 declined, and it does not move with the cap (128 and 256 give
the same) or with `fusedCeiling` (2000 gives the same). It moves with `methodByteBudget`:

  methodByteBudget   INT add chain fuses to / declines at   (cap 256)
  4000               63 / 64    (the search's floor: the real stop is at or below it)
  8000 (default)     89 / 90
  16000              170 / 171
  32000              199 / 200  (the search's ceiling: the real stop is at or above it)
  60000              199 / 200  (the same ceiling)
  0 (no byte budget) 63 / 64    (the node budget, MAX_FUSED_NODES = 64, applies instead)

So the chain stops where the loop method's bytecode reaches the budget, about 90 bytes of
code a level for this chain at the default 8000 (8000 / 89). `VarkaEmitBudget.HUGE_METHOD_LIMIT`
is also 8000, HotSpot's limit above which a method is not compiled by the JIT; that this budget
exists for that reason is read from the constant's name, not tested here. With no byte budget the
node budget binds instead, at 63 levels, as it does only in that form.

Every explanation of the first run came back in under 800 ms (the first, 721 ms, is class loading),
the rest under 200 ms, and a declined one under 40 ms, at every depth up to 64, which is 2^64
paths for a chain of fan-out two.

What this means for row 629: the cap, not a second budget, is what a query meets at the depths
the plan's rows need; a cap of 64 fuses an INT add chain to 63 levels under a predicate. The
depth to which a chain fuses is a count of IR height, not of SQL nesting: an interval `abs`
level is about two to three levels of height (inferred from the stop depths, not counted).

Not measured: the decline site of the byte budget (the explain path gives one note), the emitted class size and the cache hit of a chain that
fuses at these depths, and the plan's decimal chains.
