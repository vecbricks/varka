# The harness's own reach filter (VARKA-526), 30 October 2026, laptop numbers: AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX
# PRO 370, the three harness suites in one JVM under dev/varka_matrix.sh --runs-only, one JVM at a
# time, with other agents' JVMs on the machine (1-minute load 6 to 10 throughout), so the runs
# scatter by several seconds. Seconds are the suites' JUnit times; every run passed every test.
# The store and how the runs find it are in VARKA-528-plans-once.txt.
# The filter measured here was left out of the change that brought rows 527 and 528, so the
# code this file names (the outcomes in the store, VarkaTestReach.sameCompile, the harness
# suites in VarkaTestReach.UNRECORDED) is not in the tree.

What is stored: each check's outcome (findings, batches answered and declined, residual outputs,
Spark's true rows, every batch's status), written by the defaults' run for a check under the
defaults' options, keyed by the check's kind, context, bound expressions, rows and settings, the
rows per batch included. Under a configuration a check compiles its expressions at the defaults
too and takes the defaults' outcome where the compiled form and every kernel's bytes are the
same (VarkaTestReach.sameCompile); it runs where they differ, under a configuration of
VarkaTestReach.FULL, and where it sets its own options (the planted checks, the domains).

The planted arm (VarkaHarnessStoreSuite, "a configuration that moves one expression's kernel
runs that check and no other"): under ANSI, checkIntOverflow=false moves i + 1's kernel and not
year(d)'s; the check of i + 1 runs (moved 1) and year(d)'s takes the defaults' outcome (same
bytes 1), with Spark asked for one answer; under exactRefusalStatus=true both run.

The configurations' tallies, the store written by a defaults run of the three suites:
  cse=false                plans taken 6984   answers taken 1832 computed 41
                           checks same bytes 8921 moved 1517 unrecorded 37
  checkIntOverflow=false   plans taken 6984   answers taken 645 computed 41
                           checks same bytes 10108 moved 329 unrecorded 39
  The 37 and 39 checks the store lacked, and the 41 answers, were keyed by AnyTimeType, whose
  toString is its identity and so differs between JVMs; another pairing of runs found none
  (same bytes 8955, moved 1520, unrecorded 0 under cse=false). The key now prints a Scala object
  by its class; the runs above predate that.

So on the laptop the filter does not pay for itself in a configuration's run once the plans and
the answers are stored: the coverage harness took 18.6 s with plans and answers and 21.0 s with
the filter on, by the means of the runs each. A skipped check still compiles and emits its
kernels twice, under the configuration and at the defaults, and the store it reads is larger by
10409 outcomes (14.4 MB gzipped against 11.3 MB). What the filter saves - the kernels' class
definition, the batches and the runs - is less than that second compile here. Storing the
defaults' compiled form and kernel bytes in the outcome, so a configuration compiles once, is
the change that would let it pay; it was not built.

What the row's step (2) saves is the Build's recording of the three suites, which they now
leave out (VarkaTestReach.UNRECORDED): a defaults run recording the reach (-Dvarka.matrix
.reachRecord) and writing the store wrote no line for them (the record holds its header and the
JVM-wide line alone), and took
  defaults, no recording, no store       49.1 + 3.6 + 3.5
  defaults, recording and writing        55.8 + 3.6 + 3.8
against VARKA-509-step4.txt's 45 s plain and 92 s recording for the same three suites before.
Under a configuration the matrix now runs every harness test, since the record has no line for
them; with the store, each configuration's three suites take the seconds above.

The Build's and the matrix's hours on CI were not measured: the workflows do not yet pass the
store between the Build and the matrix's bins, which this row leaves to the CI wiring.
