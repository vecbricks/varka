# Where the option matrix's eleven costliest suites spend their time: JFR and HotSpot counter
# profiles, LAPTOP NUMBERS (24 cores, JDK 25.0.4.1), 29 October 2026, on branch
# varka-suite-profile off master 7e66c14cfb3. Each run is one runner JVM as the matrix's bins start
# it: dev/varka_matrix.sh --runs-only --skip-build --module <m> --suites <S>, with
# -Dvarka.series.size=4 as varka-option-matrix.yml passes, either --defaults or --config cse=false
# (a configuration the reach filter lets through less than most; locally there is no Build
# record, reach.tsv, so a SQL suite runs every test under it - an upper bound for those). The
# laptop was shared with other agents: each run held one of the two heavy locks, and the
# 1-minute load average at its start and end is in the run log below.
#
# Instruments, all in the JDK:
#   -XX:StartFlightRecording=settings=profile,jdk.CPUTimeSample#enabled=true,
#     jdk.CPUTimeSample#throttle=10ms,jdk.Compilation#threshold=0ms, stackdepth=512
#   -XX:+PerfDataSaveToFile: HotSpot's sun.cls.* counters (define, verify, link, init time), the
#     JIT's java.ci.totalTime and the GC's thread CPU, read from the saved file at exit.
# A Java-thread sample is put in the bucket of the leaf-most frame a rule matches (kernel class,
# ClassLoader.defineClass/reflection, java.lang.classfile and the emitter, test checks and the
# reference evaluators, data generators, the Varka compiler and IR, janino, Spark's analyzer and
# optimizer, other Spark). The CPU-time sampler drops samples it cannot walk; on these suites the
# drops are the test thread's time inside the VM - class definition, verification, linking,
# safepoints - and they are reported as "lost", with the class counters beside them saying how
# much of it was class work. JIT and GC are CPU on their own threads, which on the laptop's 24
# cores mostly run beside the test thread; on a matrix bin (2 JVMs on a 4-core runner) they take
# the test thread's cores, so their CPU is listed with the rest rather than folded away.

## 2. What each suite is bound by

Definition and GC bound - VarkaSmallTreeSuite at the defaults, VarkaVariantSuite.
  VarkaKernelChecks.runOne made a VarkaGeneratedClassLoader and defined the kernel's class for
  every run: once per body (the batch's own and the other one, otherBody) of every batch. A tree
  of size 3 or less meets four batches, so its one kernel was defined eight times; a size-4 tree,
  one batch, twice; a variant, twice. The census below counts it. At the defaults SmallTree
  defined 360706 classes; class loading took 39.1 s of the test thread (define 19.2, verify 13.3,
  link 17.9, nested), and the 325134 loaders it dropped cost the GC 139.1 s of CPU and 22.4 s of
  pauses (146 young collections, 60 remark and cleanup pauses unloading classes). 70.0 s of the
  test thread's CPU was in the VM where the sampler cannot walk; the Java samples it kept are
  emission (12.7 s) and IR (4.0 s), with kernel execution 3.9 s and the checks 1.4 s.
  VarkaVariantSuite at the defaults: 78785 classes, cls.time 11.7 s of a 34.9 s suite, GC 22.1 s
  of CPU.
  The JIT does not warm kernels: no kernel class reached the JIT in SmallTree or Variant (each
  class runs a few times and is dropped). Their JIT time is the emitter's and the checks' own
  warm-up, the same in every JVM: in SmallTree Slots.plan alone compiled 14 times at tier 4,
  10.2 s, each recompile after an ordinary unstable_if or profile_predicate deoptimization.

Emission bound - VarkaSmallTreeSuite under a configuration.
  The reach filter (VarkaReach.ranAtDefaults) emits every tree under the configuration and again
  under the defaults and runs it only where the bytes differ: under cse=false 141869 trees emitted
  the same bytes and 7902 moved. Bytecode emission was 37.1 s and the IR 7.0 s of 46.9 s sampled:
  half of it is emitting the defaults' bytes, which every configuration's JVM emits again.

Varka planning bound - VarkaGroupingBoundSuite (no kernel runs at all).
  VarkaGroupingBound.runs is 32.9 s of 58.7 s sampled, and VarkaKernelPlan.of 29.8 s inside it:
  VarkaLoopEmitter.runsForTest(outputs, from, options) plans the whole projection
  (planOf(outputs, options)) and re-adds every output before `from` for each start index, so a
  shape of n outputs is planned n times per call, and the suite calls runs three times a shape
  (account with the prediction, then test 2 with and without it). Recursive record hashCode on
  IR trees (ColumnRef, IntArith) is 3.3 s more under the same calls.

Check bound - VarkaEmitterNarrowArithSuite.
  The 2^24-row slices: the row-by-row checks are 13.1 s of 28.0 s sampled (StoredNarrowLane
  narrow, got, putAt), VarkaVectorIR.laneType and its type switch 6.1 s, called per row and root
  from the narrow lane, and Spark's overflow messages (ErrorClassesJsonReader) 2.2 s for the ANSI
  oracle. Few classes (5185); JIT 23.4 s.

Session bound - VarkaSparkFuzzSuite, VarkaDifferentialSuite, VarkaFloatingLaneSuite,
VarkaGoldenCorpusSuite.
  SparkFuzz: Spark's analyzer and optimizer are on the stack in 49.6 s and janino in 20.2 s of
  136.1 s sampled; its compositions test is 76.9 s of the suite. Differential: one test, a hundred
  queries through two sessions, is 23.0 s of 48.1. FloatingLane: 137 session tests near 0.35 s
  each. GoldenCorpus: Spark execution 40.7 s of 59.4 sampled, three .sql files 57.2 s of 66.5.
  This is rows 509, 510, 517 step 2, 527 and 528's direction and is not repeated here.

JIT bound - VarkaSeveralKernelsSuite, VarkaCodegenGiveUpSuite, VarkaCoverageCompositionFuzzSuite.
  SeveralKernels: 185.1 s of JIT for an 85.2 s suite, its largest single item janino's generated
  processNext for the 800-entry projections at tier 4, 15.1 s; CodegenGiveUp 116.5 s of JIT for
  37.8 s; the composition fuzzer 89.5 s at the defaults, where its C2-history test is 32.6 s of
  57.4 (row 517 runs that test only under the four configurations that change C2's input). On a
  24-core laptop the compile threads run beside the test thread; on a bin's 4-core runner shared
  by two JVMs they do not, which is why these suites cost more there than here.

## 3. Kernels defined, and how many repeat bytes the JVM already defined

A java.lang.instrument agent (scratch, not committed) hashed each class defined under
org.apache.spark.sql.varka.execution with its own name replaced in the constant pool, so classes
that differ only in their counter-made names hash alike. One run each at the defaults, series size
3 (the pull request's), before the change below:

suite                  defined   distinct bytes   same name, same bytes   other name, same bytes
VarkaSmallTreeSuite      74288             8266                   65002                     1020
VarkaVariantSuite        75371            29619                   37684                     8068

So 87.5% of SmallTree's and 50.0% of Variant's definitions are the same class defined again under
the same name - the per-run definition of section 2 - and only 1.4% and 10.7% are identical
bytes under a new name, which a cache keyed on the name's bytes cannot share and which is too
little to need a name-free cache. (The agent took the outermost suite frame, so it attributes all
of a suite's definitions to its run method rather than per test.)

## 4. The prototype: a checked kernel runs on its loaded class

VarkaKernelChecks.runOne keeps, per thread, the kernel it loaded last under its own loader and
runs a later call with the same name and equal bytes on it; other bytes release that loader and
define theirs (matched on the bytes, because a planted fault rewrites a kernel and keeps its
name; a class that fails to load is not kept). An emitted kernel holds no state between calls -
its only fields are the static final range tables its initialiser fills. Switchable for the
measurement: -Dvarka.checks.reuseLoaded=false is "before". By the census in section 3 this
leaves SmallTree at size 3 about 9286 definitions of its 74288 (one per kernel, 74288 - 65002),
and at size 4 one per tree, about 150000 of 360706 - arithmetic from the counts, not measured
again; VarkaKernelReuseSuite's counter test measures it for one kernel (four batches, two bodies,
one definition; a copy of the bytes, none; other bytes under the same name, one more).

Alternating before and after runs, three each, laptop seconds of the suite (JUnit XML), size 4,
with the 1-minute load average at each run's start and end. Two streams ran at once, one per
heavy lock: SmallTree at the defaults on one, the other two on the other.

## 5. Fixes, ranked by the suite-seconds they would save

Estimates scale the laptop share a fix removes to the matrix: "per run" is against the median
seconds a configuration (run 40658422887, suite-seconds.tsv on varka-508-weights), "hours" against
the suite's configuration hours in run 40855387471 (m8/results/VARKA-517-reach-gaps.txt). A bin's
runner has fewer cores than the laptop, so a fix that removes GC and JIT work beside the test
thread saves at least its laptop share there.

1. Run a checked kernel on its loaded class (VarkaKernelChecks.runOne). Prototyped; section 4.
   Measured: 57.3 s of a 162.7 s SmallTree defaults run (35%), 14.3 s of 33.6 s for Variant (43%),
   and nothing outside the spread for SmallTree under cse=false. In the matrix: Variant runs every
   test under every configuration, so some 26 of its 60.3 s a configuration and 0.35 of its 0.81
   hours; SmallTree's defaults unit, the series without the reach filter, a third of it once a
   matrix run; under configurations SmallTree is emission bound (fix 3) and gains little.
   VarkaIrFuzzSuite defines each draw's kernel twice the same way (9539 definitions of 4886 names
   in the class-load log) and should gain like Variant; not measured. A class per run made the
   GC unload hundreds of thousands of loaders on other threads, which a bin's two cores a JVM
   cannot hide, so the matrix should gain at least the laptop share.
2. Plan a projection once per runsForTest call, not once per start index, and share the runs
   between VarkaGroupingBoundSuite's first two tests. VarkaKernelPlan.of is 29.8 s of 58.7 s
   sampled at the defaults; planned three times a shape instead of three times per output, it
   all but goes: about half of the suite, some 40 of its 83.0 s a configuration and 0.5 of its
   1.07 hours. Small: a test-only entry point in VarkaLoopEmitter that takes the plan, held equal
   to the per-index one over the suite's shapes. Not built (the brief's top fix only).
3. Give the reach filter the defaults' bytes instead of emitting them again in every
   configuration's JVM (VarkaSmallTreeSuite, and VarkaIrFuzzSuite's draws the same way). Under
   cse=false half of the 44.1 s of emission and IR sampled is the defaults' copy, 22 s of a 60.0 s
   suite: some 42 of its 113.7 s a configuration and 0.7 of its 1.93 hours. Not small:
   the defaults' hashes per tree must reach every bin from one place (a record the Build writes,
   like reach.tsv), and a stale record would skip a tree the configuration moved.
4. Read each root's lane once per check, not once per row (VarkaKernelChecks' narrow lane):
   laneType and its type switch are 6.1 s of VarkaEmitterNarrowArithSuite's 28.0 s sampled, about
   a fifth of the suite: some 13 of its 67.5 s and 0.18 of its 0.88 hours. Small.
5. Session-bound suites (VarkaSparkFuzzSuite, VarkaDifferentialSuite, VarkaFloatingLaneSuite,
   VarkaGoldenCorpusSuite): the analyzer, optimizer, janino and execution of a local session per
   query. Rows 509, 510, 517 step 2, 527 and 528 own moving these answers below the session.
6. JIT warm-up per JVM (VarkaSeveralKernelsSuite, VarkaCodegenGiveUpSuite, the composition
   fuzzer, and every suite's emitter: Slots.plan's ten-odd tier-4 compiles): fewer, longer JVMs
   or a JDK AOT cache of the emitter's profiles. Measured here, not estimated; outside this
   brief, and another agent's JVM experiments cover the cache.

What the profile rules out: the JIT warming each new kernel (no kernel class of SmallTree or
Variant reached the JIT), test data generation (under 1% of every suite's samples) and a
name-free kernel cache across names (1.4% and 10.7% of definitions, section 3).
