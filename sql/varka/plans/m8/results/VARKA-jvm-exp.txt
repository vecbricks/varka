# Can an option-matrix bin run its test JVMs faster: more JVMs at once on a runner, another
# GC or heap, JIT flags, or a class archive for start-up? Laptop numbers, 29-30 October 2026,
# as a proxy for a 4-vCPU, 16 GB GitHub runner. Measured at master fa83ea5379e (the compiled
# worktree varka-509-step2e). Scripts and raw logs: /home/max/proj/.varka-tmp/jvm-exp/
# (drive.py runs a workload, analyze.py makes the tables, runs/<arm>-r<k>/summary.json per run).

== Method

Machine: Max's laptop, 12 cores / 24 threads (lscpu -e: CPUs 0-11 are cores, 12-23 their SMT
siblings; 0-3 are the 5.1 GHz cores). JDK 25.0.4.1. The laptop JVM picks UseAVX=3,
MaxVectorSize=64, where the runners report UseAVX=2, MaxVectorSize=32; every arm runs the same
laptop default, so only ratios between arms carry over to a runner, not seconds.

Pinning: the driver sets its affinity to CPUs 0-3 (four sibling-free cores) and every JVM it
starts inherits it; HotSpot then sees 4 CPUs (PrintFlagsFinal: ParallelGCThreads=4,
CICompilerCount=3). Arms s2/s4 pin to 0,1,12,13 instead: two cores with their SMT siblings.

Each unit runs exactly as a bin runs it: dev/varka_matrix.sh --skip-build --runs-only
--module M --suites S, --defaults or --config C with -Dvarka.matrix.reachFile (a reach file a
CI Build wrote on 29 October, the same file in every arm, with lines for SQL suites only, so
the catalyst units run every test under a configuration), --jvm-arg -Dvarka.series.size=4, the
build's jvm.opts (-Xmx4g, G1 by default), the sanitizer and checkBothBodies on. Each unit is
wrapped in /usr/bin/time -v (CPU = user + sys of the JVM, peak RSS = its maxrss); a sampler
sums the RSS of all the run's JVMs every 0.5 s for the peak total.

Workload, 10 units, launched in this order, N at a time:
  u0 defaults|catalyst|VarkaEmitterNarrowArithSuite                    (own JVM)
  u1 cse=false|catalyst|VarkaSmallTreeSuite                            (own JVM)
  u2 defaults|catalyst|VarkaGroupingBoundSuite
  u3 cse=false|catalyst|VarkaGroupingBoundSuite
  u4 cse=false|sql|VarkaDifferentialSuite                              (reach: 7 of 27 run)
  u5 defaults|sql|VarkaFloatingLaneSuite,VarkaProjectExecSuite,VarkaFilterRaiseOrderSuite
  u6 division=DOUBLE_DIV|sql|VarkaFloatingLaneSuite,VarkaLongLaneSuite (reach: 0 of 171 run)
  u7 division=DOUBLE_DIV|catalyst|VarkaEmitterChronoSuite,VarkaExpressionCompilerSuite
  u8 cse=false|sql|VarkaFloatingLaneSuite                              (reach: 45 of 137 run)
  u9 division=DOUBLE_DIV|catalyst|VarkaEmitterDivisionSuite            (own JVM)
u6 runs no test at all, so its wall is a JVM's fixed cost: start, SparkSession, suite set-up.

Rounds: rounds 1 to 3 ran all twelve main arms once each, the order rotated per round
(rounds.sh). The AOT and SMT arms ran after them (extra.sh), and rounds 4 and 5 ran c2,
c2-aot, c2-cds, s2 and c4 interleaved (block.sh) - see "Noise" for why.

Lock: each run held one of the two shared heavy slots (whichever freed first) for its whole
duration; other agents used the other slot throughout. 1-minute load average at the start of
the 55 timed runs: 3.2 to 12.7, median 6.6 (recorded per run in summary.json).

Answers: every run of every arm passed, and every unit's "Tests:" lines (succeeded, failed,
canceled) equal the c2 arm's - e.g. u0 30 succeeded / 19 canceled, u5 173 succeeded, u7 193
succeeded / 27 canceled, in every arm. No arm changed what was tested.

== Noise

The same arm moved by up to a quarter between periods of the night, more than most effects
measured: c2 took 225, 226 and 183 s in rounds 1 to 3 and 184 and 179 s in rounds 4 and 5;
c2-cds took 220, 223 and 237 s, then 171 and 171 s. The load average does not explain it
(other agents' work on CPUs 0-3, the package's turbo budget and memory traffic are not in
it). The first AOT runs looked 24% faster than c2, but they ran in the fast period after
01:25; rounds 4 and 5, interleaved, show CDS and AOT equal and c2 about 6% slower. So below,
differences come from runs adjacent in time (same round), and an effect under about 5% is
called unreadable.

== 1. Concurrency (G1, -Xmx4g)

s2 and s4 r1 and r2 ran between r3 and r4), then CPU/wall and the ratio to c2:

s2   2    0,1,12,13  219  220  -    219  217  744            3.39      r4 1.19, r5 1.21
s4   4    0,1,12,13  227  225  -    -    -    855            3.78      s4/s2 1.04 1.02

Peak RSS: largest single JVM 2.79 GB (any arm), largest sum of all JVMs at once 4.32 GB (c4,
four JVMs). Everything fits 16 GB with room to spare at any N up to 4.

- One JVM alone keeps 1.9 CPUs busy (Spark's local threads, the JIT, G1). Two JVMs already
  take 3.1 of 4 CPUs (rounds 1 to 3), so a third or fourth JVM can add at most the last 0.9.
- On four real cores, 4 JVMs finish the workload 2 to 12% sooner than 2 in four rounds of
  five and 3% later in one (median 0.97), for 2 to 16% more CPU (median 1.09): each unit
  runs 1.2 to 2.2 times slower beside three others than beside one.
- On two cores with their SMT siblings, which is how we assume a 4-vCPU Azure runner is laid
  out (platform.txt does not record threads per core), 4 JVMs gain nothing over 2 (1.04,
  1.02) and burn 15% more CPU (855 against 744 s). 2 JVMs take 20% longer there than on four
  real cores.
- Verdict: keep 2 JVMs a runner. The gain on real cores is at the noise floor and disappears
  on the SMT layout the runners most likely have.

== 2. GC and heap (2 JVMs unless named)

arm      flags                     runs  wall s median (range)  vs same-round reference
c2-par   -XX:+UseParallelGC        3     214 (202-219)          vs c2: 0.90 0.95 1.20
c2-ser   -XX:+UseSerialGC          3     222 (192-229)          vs c2: 1.02 0.98 1.05
c4-ser   -XX:+UseSerialGC, 4 JVMs  3     203 (199-222)          vs c4: 1.03 0.98 1.19
c4-x2g   -Xmx2g, 4 JVMs            3     215 (182-215)          vs c4: 1.09 1.05 0.97

- ParallelGC: 5% faster in two rounds, 20% slower in the third - unreadable, no consistent
  gain. SerialGC: equal to G1 within noise; it does cut the largest JVM's RSS from 2.34 to
  1.25 GB (largest JVM, rounds 1 to 3), which nothing needs since memory is not the limit.
- -Xmx2g: nothing to gain. A smaller heap would only let more JVMs fit, and 4 JVMs at -Xmx4g
  peaked at 4.32 GB together; at 4 JVMs it was 3% faster to 9% slower than -Xmx4g.

== 3. JIT

- -XX:TieredStopAtLevel=1 (C1 only), one JVM, one run each (t1-r1, t1base-r1):
  VarkaEmitterNarrowArithSuite 153 s against 32 s with C2 (4.8 times slower; the Vector API
  needs C2's intrinsics), VarkaDifferentialSuite under cse=false 16 s against 13 s. Dropped.
- -XX:CICompilerCount=2 at 4 JVMs (c4-cic2, 3 runs): 202 s median, against c4 in the same
  round 1.06, 0.99, 1.05. No gain from fewer compiler threads under concurrency.
- -XX:+UseCompactObjectHeaders at 2 JVMs (c2-coh, 3 runs): 231 s median, against c2 1.04,
  0.95, 1.27. No gain; its RSS saving (largest JVM 2.25 against c2's 2.34 GB in rounds 1 to
  3) is not needed.

== 4. Start-up

On CI (the 154 bins of a fanned-out run from 29 October, /home/max/proj/.varka-tmp/w508,
ci_overhead.py): 1540 JVMs, 89855 JVM-seconds. The catalyst JVMs (1421) take 11 s at the
median and 7 s at p10, 2 s the shortest; the SQL JVMs (119) 273 s at the median. A JVM's
seconds outside its JUnit suite times (start, discovery, exit) are 1.7 s at the median; each
suite's set-up outside its tests is 0.8 s (catalyst) and 7.3 s (SQL) at the median. 451 of
the 1540 JVMs (392 catalyst, 59 SQL) ran no test, the reach having skipped every one: they took
13 s at the median, 7 s at p10 - the fixed cost of a JVM on a runner.

On the laptop the fixed cost of a JVM (u6, no test run) is 5.8 s alone and 7.2 to 7.4 s at 2
JVMs (rounds 4 and 5). From launch to the first suite's JUnit timestamp takes 1.3 s at the
median and 2.7 s at p90 over all runs (the timestamp has 1 s resolution, and the JUnit-class
suites carry none), so most of a JVM's fixed cost falls inside its first suite - the
SparkSession and the loading of Spark's and Varka's classes - not in the JVM's own start;
which is why a class archive helps through a run and not only at its start.

Class archives: the build's test classpath holds 11 class directories (the test-classes of
sql/core, catalyst and nine more), and both CDS (-XX:ArchiveClassesAtExit) and the AOT cache
(-XX:AOTCacheOutput) refuse to dump with "Error: non-empty directory '...test-classes'". With
each directory zipped into a jar (cds/jarify.sh, 80 MB of jars), both dump from one training
JVM (cse=false, VarkaFloatingLaneSuite and VarkaDifferentialSuite with
VarkaGroupingBoundSuite, 166 tests, 80 s): a 184 MB dynamic CDS archive and a 201 MB AOT
cache. As READING_LEYDEN.md expected, the AOT cache holds no linked classes and no profiles
with the incubator module on the module path: "instance classes = 16992, aot-linked = 0",
"KlassTrainingData = 0", "MethodTrainingData = 0", "Full module graph = disabled". It is a
class archive like CDS. For u7 the archives serve most classes (-Xlog:class+load):
jars alone 1451 of 11230 classes from the JDK's archive; CDS 8917 of 10937; AOT 7947 of 11215.

c2-aot   jars + AOT cache  171  171  173  170  172  r4 0.92, r5 0.96 (r1-r3 ran after round 3)

Rounds 4 and 5 (interleaved, the only rounds with all three of c2, CDS and AOT):
  c2 184 / 179 s, CPU 605 / 589;  c2-cds 171 / 171 s, CPU 559 / 559;
  c2-aot 170 / 172 s, CPU 551 / 554.
Per unit in rounds 4 and 5 (2 JVMs), class directories / CDS / AOT: u6 7.4 and 7.2 s /
5.4 and 5.4 / 5.2 and 5.6; u9 5.6 and 5.8 / 5.2 and 5.0 / 5.2 and 5.2; u7 12.8 and 13.4 /
11.8 and 11.6 / 11.6 and 11.8.

- Jars alone change nothing (0.99 to 1.10, inside the noise).
- CDS and the AOT cache are equal to each other and save 4 to 7% of the workload's wall and
  5 to 8% of its CPU (rounds 4 and 5): about 2 s on a JVM that runs no test (u6), 0.5 to 1.5
  s on the others. Rounds 1 to 3 show CDS within noise of c2, so the saving is at the
  edge of what this laptop can resolve; it is consistent in the two interleaved rounds.
- Scaled to the matrix: about 2 s of fixed cost saved per JVM (a laptop figure; a runner core
  is slower, so likely more there). At 300 to 400 units, about 10 to 13 runner-minutes over
  the whole matrix; at the 1540 JVMs of the 29 October run, about 51 minutes of 1498 (3.4%). A
  bin's wall falls by about 2 s times its JVMs over its two lanes, i.e. 10 to 40 s on a bin
  of 10 to 40 JVMs; the catalyst-heavy bins (11 s JVMs) gain the most, up to a fifth.
- Costs on CI, not measured here: the build job must zip the 11 class directories and run a
  training JVM (about 80 s on the laptop), and each bin must download a 184 to 201 MB archive
  besides the jars. CDS checks each jar's size and modification time against the archive, so
  the bins must see the very jars the build archived (an artifact that resets mtimes would
  make every JVM refuse the archive; with -Xshare:on it fails loudly, which is what to use).
  The archive is tied to the JDK build, which the runners share. The test-classes jars also
  hold the copied test resources: these ten units passed on jars, the matrix's other suites
  were never run on them, and a suite that turns a resource URL into a File would break on a
  jar: URL - so the CI A/B is also the test of the jarred classpath.

== Recommendation

Try on CI:
- A CDS archive (or the AOT cache: equal here, and the AOT cache is the JDK's way forward) of
  the jarred test classpath, made in the build job and used by every bin with -Xshare:on (or
  -XX:AOTMode=on). Expected: about 2 s a JVM, 3 to 7% of the matrix's JVM time, most in the
  bins of many short catalyst JVMs; to be confirmed against the archive's download time per
  bin. Measure it as an A/B on two matrix runs of the same commit.

Drop:
- 3 or 4 JVMs a runner: at best 1 to 12% on four real cores, nothing on the SMT layout the
  runners most likely have, for 2 to 24% more CPU. Worth one check only: record lscpu's
  threads per core in the bins' platform step; if a runner turns out to have four real cores,
  3 JVMs could be retried.
- ParallelGC, SerialGC, -Xmx2g: no consistent difference from G1 at -Xmx4g; memory is not
  the limit (4.32 GB peak for four JVMs).
- -XX:TieredStopAtLevel=1 (4.8 times slower on a Vector API suite), -XX:CICompilerCount=2,
  -XX:+UseCompactObjectHeaders: no gain.
